Anchored in M. Laxmikanth's Indian Polity and Shivam Shankar Singh's How to Win an Indian Election.

The Constitution's first 23 articles on the Union read like a textbook on the President's powers. But the lived Constitution is different: the President is a constitutional head who acts on advice; real executive authority sits with the Prime Minister and the Council of Ministers, who are collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha. Understanding the gap between the text and the practice is the whole game.

Reading time: ~22 min Pairs with: Laxmikanth, Ch. 17–21 · D.D. Basu, Ch. on the Executive · Granville Austin (1999), Ch. on the Presidency

1 · The President of India (Articles 52–62)

The President is the constitutional head of the Union and the formal repository of the Union's executive power (Article 53). The Constitution calls the President the "first citizen of India."

1.1 Eligibility

A candidate must be: - A citizen of India; - At least 35 years old; - Qualified to be a member of the Lok Sabha; - Not holding any office of profit under the Union, a State, or any local/public authority.

1.2 The Electoral College

The President is elected indirectly by an Electoral College comprising:

  1. The elected members of both Houses of Parliament (Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha);
  2. The elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of all 28 States;
  3. The elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of the UTs of Delhi, Puducherry, and Jammu & Kashmir.

Nominated MPs and MLAs do not vote. MLCs (members of Legislative Councils) also do not vote (source).

1.3 The voting system: STV with weighted values

Voting is by Single Transferable Vote on the principle of Proportional Representation, by secret ballot (Article 55(3)). Every elector ranks candidates in order of preference.

But each elector's vote does not count the same. The Constitution requires uniformity of representation between states (proportional to population) and parity between the Union and the States as a whole. So vote values are calculated:

The two formulas
Value of one MLA's vote = (Population of State as per 1971 Census ÷ Number of elected MLAs of that State) ÷ 1000
Value of one MP's vote = (Total value of all MLA votes of all States) ÷ (Total number of elected MPs of both Houses)

The 1971 census continues to be used — frozen by the 42nd Amendment (1976) and extended by the 84th Amendment to "the first census after 2026" (source).

Worked examples:

State 1971 population Elected MLAs MLA vote value
Uttar Pradesh 8,38,49,905 403 8,38,49,905 ÷ 403 ÷ 1000 ≈ 208
Sikkim 2,09,843 32 2,09,843 ÷ 32 ÷ 1000 ≈ 7
Delhi (UT) 40,65,698 70 ≈ 58

1.4 The 2022 election

The 2022 Electoral College totalled 4,809 voters with an aggregate vote value of 10,86,431 (after deducting the dissolved J&K Assembly). The MP vote value was 700.

Candidate Votes Vote value
Droupadi Murmu (NDA) 2,161 5,77,777
Yashwant Sinha (Opposition) 1,058 2,61,062

Murmu became the 15th President of India on 25 July 2022 — the first member of a Scheduled Tribe and the second woman to hold the office, and the first President born after independence (source).

1.5 Term, oath, and powers

Term: 5 years from the date of assumption; eligible for re-election (Article 57). The oath is administered by the Chief Justice of India (Article 60).

Powers of the President are formally vast but, by Article 74, exercisable only on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers:

  • Executive — appoints PM, Council of Ministers, Governors, AG, CAG, CEC and ECs, Chairpersons of UPSC/Finance Commission, Ambassadors.
  • Legislative — summons and prorogues Parliament; dissolves the Lok Sabha; assents to Bills; promulgates ordinances when Parliament is not in session (Article 123); nominates 12 members to the Rajya Sabha.
  • Judicial — pardons, reprieves, respites, remissions; can commute death sentences (Article 72).
  • Financial — Money Bills can be introduced only on the President's prior recommendation; constitutes the Finance Commission every five years.
  • Diplomatic — international treaties and agreements are negotiated in the President's name.
  • Military — Supreme Commander of the Armed Forces.
  • Emergency — National Emergency (Article 352), President's Rule in a State (Article 356), Financial Emergency (Article 360).

1.6 Impeachment (Article 61)

The President can be removed only for "violation of the Constitution." The procedure:

  1. The motion can be introduced in either House.
  2. The notice must be signed by at least one-fourth of the total membership of that House.
  3. 14 days' written notice must be given.
  4. The initiating House must pass the resolution by a special majority — 2/3 of total membership.
  5. The other House investigates and must also pass by 2/3 of total membership.
  6. Nominated MPs participate in impeachment (unlike presidential elections).

No Indian President has ever been impeached.

2 · The Vice-President (Articles 63–73)

The Vice-President is the ex officio Chairperson of the Rajya Sabha (Article 64) and discharges the President's functions during a vacancy (Article 65).

2.1 The Electoral College — different from the President's

Crucial distinction: the VP is elected by the members of both Houses of Parliament — both elected AND nominated. State legislators do not vote. The method is again STV/PR by secret ballot.

2.2 Removal — uniquely "effective majority"

Article 67(b): a resolution of the Rajya Sabha, passed by a majority of all the then members of the Council of States, agreed to by the Lok Sabha by simple majority. 14 days' notice required. The Vice-President is the only constitutional functionary in India removable by an "effective majority" rather than a special majority — and only the Rajya Sabha can initiate the removal motion.

2.3 The 2025 mid-term replacement

Jagdeep Dhankhar had been sworn in as the 14th Vice-President on 11 August 2022. He resigned on 21 July 2025 citing health reasons — only the third early vacancy in Indian history. A by-election was held on 9 September 2025 — the first VP by-election since 1987. C.P. Radhakrishnan (NDA candidate, former Governor of Jharkhand and Maharashtra) defeated B. Sudarshan Reddy (opposition INDIA bloc candidate), polling 452 electoral votes, and was sworn in as the 15th Vice-President of India on 12 September 2025 (source).

3 · The Prime Minister and Council of Ministers (Articles 74, 75)

3.1 Appointment and constitutional design

Article 74(1) is the core: there shall be a Council of Ministers, with the Prime Minister at the head, to "aid and advise" the President, who "shall act in accordance with such advice." (The italicised phrase was inserted by the 42nd Amendment, 1976, and reinforced by the 44th Amendment, 1978 — the President can ask for reconsideration once but, if the Council reiterates the advice, must comply.)

The PM is appointed by the President. By convention, the President invites the leader of the party or coalition commanding a majority in the Lok Sabha. Other Ministers are appointed by the President on the PM's advice. Ministers hold office during the pleasure of the President (Art. 75(2)) — but, as a matter of constitutional convention, that pleasure tracks the PM's pleasure.

3.2 Categories of ministers

The Council of Ministers comprises three tiers — a convention rather than a constitutional distinction:

  • Cabinet Ministers — senior ministers with portfolios; constitute the Cabinet (a sub-set of the Council).
  • Ministers of State with Independent Charge — hold a department but do not attend Cabinet meetings ex officio.
  • Ministers of State — assist Cabinet Ministers in specific portfolios.

3.3 Size of the Council — the 91st Amendment cap

Article 75(1A) (inserted by the 91st Amendment, 2003; effective 7 January 2004): the total number of Ministers, including the PM, shall not exceed 15 % of the total strength of the Lok Sabha (source). For a 543-member Lok Sabha, the ceiling is ~81 ministers.

A parallel cap exists for States under Article 164(1A) — 15 % of Assembly strength, subject to a minimum of 12 ministers.

The 91st Amendment also added an anti-defection safeguard: a member disqualified under the Tenth Schedule cannot be appointed Minister until re-elected.

3.4 Collective responsibility (Article 75(3))

The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha. A successful no-confidence motion brings down the entire ministry, not individual ministers. Inside the Cabinet, decisions taken by majority bind every minister — disagreement must be resolved by resignation.

3.5 Cabinet Committees

Cabinet Committees are constituted by the PM under the Government of India (Transaction of Business) Rules. After the 2024 election, the Modi government reconstituted eight Cabinet Committees on 3 July 2024, with the PM chairing six (source):

Committee Function
Cabinet Committee on Security (CCS) Defence, national security, top security appointments. Members: PM, HM, FM, DM, EAM
Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA) Overall economic policy, industrial policy, fiscal management
Cabinet Committee on Political Affairs (CCPA) The "super-Cabinet" — major political issues
Cabinet Committee on Parliamentary Affairs Government business in Parliament
Appointments Committee of the Cabinet (ACC) Top bureaucratic and PSU appointments
Cabinet Committee on Investment and Growth Large-investment clearance
Cabinet Committee on Employment and Skill Development Employment policy
Cabinet Committee on Accommodation Allotment of govt accommodation

3.6 The PMO

The Prime Minister's Office is headed by the Principal Secretary (Cabinet rank), supported by Additional Principal Secretaries, the National Security Adviser, Advisers, Joint Secretaries, and Directors. In Modi 3.0:

  • P.K. Mishra continues as Principal Secretary (1972-batch IAS, Gujarat cadre; heading PMO since 2019; Cabinet rank).
  • Ajit Doval continues as National Security Adviser (1968-batch IPS, former IB Director; Cabinet rank).

3.7 Current government (June 2026)

Narendra Modi was sworn in for his third consecutive term as Prime Minister on 9 June 2024 — only the second person after Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru to hold the post for three consecutive terms. The Council of Ministers comprises roughly 30 Cabinet Ministers, 5 Ministers of State (Independent Charge), and 36 Ministers of State — a total of about 72 ministers, well within the 15 % ceiling. (Composition may have shifted with subsequent reshuffles.)

4 · The Attorney General (Article 76)

The Attorney General of India (AG) is the highest law officer of the country. Appointed by the President; must be qualified to be a Supreme Court judge. The AG:

  • Holds office during the pleasure of the President (no fixed tenure).
  • Has the right of audience in all courts of India.
  • May participate in Parliament's proceedings but cannot vote.

Current AG (2026): R. Venkataramani, appointed 1 October 2022, succeeding K.K. Venugopal. Reappointed for a two-year term on 1 October 2025, extending his tenure to 30 September 2027 (source).

The AG is supported by the Solicitor General and Additional Solicitors General — these are statutory law officers under the Law Officers (Conditions of Service) Rules, not constitutional offices.

5 · Where the real power sits

The Constitution distributes Union executive authority across three roles:

President

Constitutional head. Has formal authority for every executive act. In practice, acts on Cabinet's advice. Reserve powers in crisis: dissolving LS, recommending President's Rule, granting pardons, choosing a PM in a hung house.

Prime Minister

Real executive. Chairs Cabinet. Holds the political loyalty of the Lok Sabha majority. Appoints/removes ministers, allocates portfolios, sets the agenda. Often described as "first among equals" — but in a strong-mandate government, very much first.

Council of Ministers

The collective body that takes binding decisions. Tied together by the doctrine of collective responsibility — if you can't agree, you resign. The Cabinet is the working sub-set; CoMs and MoSs assist.
