Why this guide exists

You wanted to understand India's government end-to-end: what the Constitution actually says, how Parliament works, how the Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha differ, how the President, the Prime Minister, the MLAs, the Governors are picked, what the electoral math really is, and — crucially — how a campaign is actually executed on the ground, booth by booth.

This guide is built in two layers:

  1. The System Architecture — what M. Laxmikanth's Indian Polity teaches: the constitutional structure, the offices, the rules.
  2. The Ground-Level Execution — what Shivam Shankar Singh's How to Win an Indian Election teaches: how parties actually identify, persuade, and turn out voters.

Every non-trivial fact is sourced. Primary references are listed at the bottom of each chapter and consolidated in the final References chapter.

How to read this guide

The chapters are ordered to build understanding from the foundations upward:

Foundations (Ch. 1–4)

The Constitution, your rights, and the federal idea — the ground everything else stands on.

Union Government (Ch. 5–8)

The Centre: President, PM, Cabinet, the two Houses, and how a Bill becomes Law.

State Government (Ch. 9)

Governor, Chief Minister, MLAs — and why states matter more than people think.

Other Pillars (Ch. 10–11)

The Judiciary, the Election Commission, and the constitutional bodies that hold the system together.

Elections (Ch. 12–13)

How an Indian election actually runs — and the mathematics behind the result.

Local Government (Ch. 14)

Panchayats and Nagar Palikas — the layer closest to citizens.

Politics in Practice (Ch. 15–17)

Parties, the anti-defection law, money in politics, and ground-level campaign machinery.

Today (Ch. 18)

The 2024 Lok Sabha result and the 2025–26 state-election cycle, including West Bengal 2026.

Reference (Ch. 19)

Glossary, timeline of constitutional amendments, and the full bibliography.

The two anchor books M. Laxmikanth — Indian Polity (McGraw Hill, 7th ed.): the standard reference for the constitutional structure. Used by virtually every UPSC aspirant for a reason — it is exhaustive and well-organised. Cited throughout chapters 1–11 and 14–16.
Shivam Shankar Singh — How to Win an Indian Election (Penguin, 2019): a former political strategist's first-person account of how booths, surveys, IT cells, money, and caste arithmetic are actually wired together. Cited throughout chapters 17–18.

A note on citations

A note on dates

This build is dated June 2026. It reflects:

  • The 18th Lok Sabha constituted after the 2024 General Election (Modi III).
  • The state election cycle through Haryana (Oct 2024), J&K (Sept–Oct 2024), Maharashtra & Jharkhand (Nov 2024), Delhi (Feb 2025), Bihar (Oct–Nov 2025) and the West Bengal Assembly Election (April–May 2026).
  • The 106th Constitutional Amendment (Women's Reservation, 2023) and the CEC and Other ECs Act 2023.
  • The Supreme Court's 15 February 2024 judgment striking down the Electoral Bonds scheme.
