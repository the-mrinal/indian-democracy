### Political-ground terms (from Singh's *How to Win an Indian Election*)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Panna pramukh** | "Page chief" — a karyakarta assigned one double-sided page of the electoral roll (~60 voters) |
| **Parchi vitran** | Slip distribution — handing each voter a slip with booth/serial/section |
| **Booth pramukh** | Party's booth-level chief karyakarta |
| **Padyatra** | Foot march by a candidate or leader |
| **Karyakarta** | Party worker / volunteer |
| **Star campaigner** | Leader recognised under Section 77 RPA — travel exempt from candidate cap |
| **DBT** | Direct Benefit Transfer — to a beneficiary's bank account |
| **Hawala** | Informal value-transfer system used (illegally) for political cash |
| **Vote contractor** | Local strongman who delivers a fixed bloc of votes for fixed consideration |
| **Envelope politics** | Cash distribution to voters in the last 48 hours |
| **IT cell** | Party's digital/social-media operations team |
| **GOTV** | Get Out The Vote — turnout mobilisation |

## 3 · A timeline of constitutional moments

| Year | Event |
|---:|:---|
| **1946** | Cabinet Mission Plan; Constituent Assembly's first sitting (9 Dec) |
| **1947** | Partition; Indian Independence Act |
| **1949** | Constitution adopted (26 Nov) |
| **1950** | Constitution comes into force (26 Jan); Supreme Court inaugurated |
| **1951** | 1st Constitutional Amendment — Ninth Schedule |
| **1951–52** | First general election |
| **1956** | 7th Amendment — States Reorganisation; Linguistic Reorganisation |
| **1967** | "Aaya Ram Gaya Ram" episode in Haryana |
| **1971** | 26th Amendment — abolition of privy purses |
| **1973** | Kesavananda Bharati — Basic Structure doctrine |
| **1975–77** | Emergency |
| **1976** | 42nd Amendment — Socialist, Secular, Integrity inserted; Fundamental Duties added; Article 31C strengthened |
| **1978** | 44th Amendment — Right to Property removed from FRs; Emergency safeguards |
| **1980** | Minerva Mills — judicial review part of basic structure |
| **1985** | 52nd Amendment — Tenth Schedule (Anti-Defection) |
| **1988** | 61st Amendment — voting age 21→18 |
| **1989** | Multi-member ECI; T.N. Seshan era begins (1990) |
| **1991** | LPG reforms; 69th Amendment — Delhi as NCT |
| **1992** | 73rd & 74th Amendments — Panchayats & Municipalities |
| **1993** | 73rd & 74th Amendments come into force; PESA seeded |
| **1994** | S.R. Bommai — federalism as basic structure |
| **1996** | PESA Act |
| **2000** | Creation of Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Uttaranchal |
| **2002** | 86th Amendment — Right to Education (Art 21A); 9th Schedule reading down in *I.R. Coelho* (2007) |
| **2003** | 91st Amendment — CoM cap; Split exception removed from 10th Schedule |
| **2005** | Right to Information Act |
| **2009** | Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act (under Art 21A) |
| **2013** | NOTA (PUCL v UoI); Lily Thomas (Section 8(4) RPA struck down); Lokpal & Lokayuktas Act |
| **2014** | NJAC Amendment + Act |
| **2015** | NJAC struck down (Fourth Judges Case); NITI Aayog replaces Planning Commission |
| **2016** | 101st Amendment — GST |
| **2017** | GST live (1 July); Puttaswamy — right to privacy as FR |
| **2018** | 102nd Amendment — NCBC constitutional status; Electoral Bond Scheme operational |
| **2019** | 103rd Amendment — EWS reservation; Article 370 abrogation; J&K Reorg Act; SC strength raised to 34 |
| **2020** | 104th Amendment — SC/ST extension to 2030; Anglo-Indian seats abolished |
| **2021** | 105th Amendment — states' SEBC identification restored |
| **2023** | Anoop Baranwal (March); GNCTD Amdt Act (Aug); Article 370 abrogation upheld (Dec); 106th Amendment — Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam (Sept); CEC & Other ECs Act (Dec) |
| **2024** | Electoral Bonds struck down (15 Feb); 2024 LS election (Apr–Jun); Modi sworn in for third term (9 Jun); 18th LS constituted; Om Birla re-elected Speaker; Rahul Gandhi becomes LOP |
| **2025** | Delhi BJP government returns (Feb); State of TN v Governor (April) — 10 bills deemed assented under Art 142; VP Dhankhar resigns (Jul); CP Radhakrishnan elected 15th VP (Sept); Bihar election → NDA returned (Nov) |
| **2026** | 16th FC report tabled (Feb); Nari Shakti Vandan notified in force (16 Apr); 131st Amendment Bill defeated 298–230 (Apr); WB Assembly election → BJP first majority (Apr–May); TVK upset in TN (May); CM Suvendu Adhikari sworn in WB |
| **2027** *(scheduled)* | First post-2026 Census (1 March) — triggers delimitation + Women's Reservation operationalisation |
| **2029** *(earliest)* | First Lok Sabha election with Women's Reservation |

## 5 · Other essential references

### Books

- **Granville Austin** — *The Indian Constitution: Cornerstone of a Nation* (Oxford, 1966). Definitive history of the Constituent Assembly. Sequel: *Working a Democratic Constitution: The Indian Experience* (Oxford, 1999) — for Basic Structure, Emergency, and amendment politics.
- **D.D. Basu** — *Introduction to the Constitution of India* (LexisNexis, currently in 26th edition). The benchmark textbook for constitutional law.
- **Subhash Kashyap** — *Our Constitution: An Introduction to India's Constitution and Constitutional Law* (NBT); *Our Parliament* — authoritative on the chambers, procedure, devices and committees.
- **M.P. Singh** — *Indian Government and Politics* (Pearson). Contemporary analyses of the Council of Ministers, judicial activism, PILs, EC controversies, Centre-State frictions.
- **Madhav Khosla** — *The Indian Constitution* (OUP Very Short Introductions, 2012). Compact analytical overview.
- **Madhav Khosla & Sujit Choudhry (eds.)** — *The Oxford Handbook of the Indian Constitution* (2016). Scholarly essays.
- **A.G. Noorani** — *Constitutional Questions in India* (OUP). For Basic Structure jurisprudence and Article 356 debates.
- **Milan Vaishnav** — *When Crime Pays: Money and Muscle in Indian Politics* (Yale University Press / HarperCollins India, 2017). Canonical study of criminality in Indian politics.
- **Christophe Jaffrelot** — *Modi's India: Hindu Nationalism and the Rise of Ethnic Democracy* (Princeton, 2021); *Gujarat Under Modi* (Hurst, 2024).
- **Atul Kohli** — *Democracy and Discontent: India's Growing Crisis of Governability* (Cambridge, 1990). Foundational on Indian democratic decay narratives.
- **Yogendra Yadav** — *Making Sense of Indian Democracy* (Permanent Black, 2020). Essays on the "third electoral system" and the southern shift.
- **Pratap Bhanu Mehta** — selected essays in *Economic & Political Weekly* and *Indian Express*; *The Burden of Democracy* (Penguin, 2003).
- **Yamini Aiyar** — body of work at the Accountability Initiative, Centre for Policy Research, on PRI fiscal devolution.

## 6 · A suggested reading order if you've made it this far

If this guide is a starting point and you want to keep going:

1. **Read Laxmikanth cover-to-cover** as a structural foundation.
2. **Read Granville Austin's *Cornerstone of a Nation*** for the Constituent Assembly, then *Working a Democratic Constitution* for the post-1950 evolution.
3. **Read Singh's *How to Win an Indian Election*** in one sitting — it is short and brutally honest.
4. **Read Milan Vaishnav's *When Crime Pays*** to understand the candidate-selection logic.
5. **Sign up for PRS Legislative Research's weekly bill-tracker emails** to follow live legislation.
6. **Bookmark the ECI results portal** and check it after every state election cycle.
7. **Read Pratap Bhanu Mehta** to keep a critical eye on the gap between constitutional text and democratic practice.
