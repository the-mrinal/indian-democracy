# Recent Elections: 2024 Lok Sabha → 2026 State Cycle

<p class="lede">Two years of Indian politics, in one chapter. The 2024 Lok Sabha pulled the BJP back from a standalone majority for the first time in a decade, and produced a formally recognised Leader of Opposition again. The 2025 cycle — Delhi, Bihar — strengthened the NDA's regional reach. The April–May 2026 simultaneous state cycle delivered the most consequential single result of the decade: <strong>BJP's first-ever government in West Bengal</strong>, with Suvendu Adhikari as Chief Minister, plus a stunning newcomer win in Tamil Nadu and a UDF wave in Kerala.</p>

### 1.3 Government formation

- **Narendra Modi** sworn in as **Prime Minister for a third consecutive term on 9 June 2024** — only the second person after Pandit Nehru.
- **Om Birla** re-elected **Speaker** on 26 June 2024 — only the second person ever to serve consecutive Speaker terms (after Balram Jakhar).
- **Rahul Gandhi** sworn in as **Leader of the Opposition** — the first formally recognised LOP since 2014.

### 1.4 State-wise highlights

#### Uttar Pradesh (80 seats)

The biggest shock of 2024:

| Party | Seats |
|---:|---:|
| SP | 37 |
| BJP | 33 |
| INC | 6 |
| RLD | 2 |
| ASP-KR | 1 |
| AD(S) | 1 |

INDIA bloc total 43 vs NDA 36 ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Indian_general_election_in_Uttar_Pradesh)). The Faizabad-Ayodhya seat itself went to the Samajwadi Party — a result widely read as a rejection of campaign Hindutva symbolism in the face of livelihood concerns.

#### Maharashtra (48 seats)

The Mahayuti collapse — engineered Shiv Sena and NCP splits backfired electorally:

| Alliance | Seats |
|---:|---:|
| MVA (INC + SS-UBT + NCP-SP) | 31 |
| Mahayuti (BJP + SS-Shinde + NCP-Ajit) | 17 |

Within MVA: INC 13, SS-UBT 9, NCP-SP 8.

#### West Bengal (42 seats)

TMC defended its turf:

| Party | Seats |
|---:|---:|
| TMC | 29 |
| BJP | 12 |
| INC | 1 |

#### Tamil Nadu (39 seats)

DMK-led INDIA sweep — **DMK alone won 22**; the rest went to allies (Congress, CPI, CPI(M), VCK, MDMK, IUML).

#### Karnataka (28 seats), Andhra Pradesh (25 seats), Telangana (17 seats)

- **Karnataka**: BJP-JD(S) NDA dominant.
- **Andhra Pradesh** (simultaneous LS + Assembly): **TDP + BJP + JSP swept**; YSRCP collapsed; **Chandrababu Naidu** sworn in as CM.
- **Telangana**: Congress and BJP split the haul; KCR's BRS marginalised.

#### Kerala (20 seats)

UDF (Congress-led) dominant. **Rahul Gandhi** retained Wayanad (later resigned the seat after winning Rae Bareli too); **Shashi Tharoor** retained Thiruvananthapuram. The BJP **opened its account in Kerala** for the first time, with **Suresh Gopi** winning Thrissur.

### 2.3 Maharashtra — November 2024

Polling on **20 November 2024**; turnout **66.05 %**, the highest since 1995. Mahayuti landslide:

|---:|---:|
| Mahayuti | 235 (BJP 132 · SS-Shinde 57 · NCP-Ajit 41) |

For the first time in over six decades, **no party crossed the 10 % threshold for the Leader of Opposition** post. **Devendra Fadnavis** sworn in as Chief Minister for a third time on **5 December 2024** ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Maharashtra_Legislative_Assembly_election)).

**Rekha Gupta** (MLA, Shalimar Bagh) sworn in as Chief Minister on **20 February 2025** — Delhi's first BJP CM since **Sushma Swaraj in 1998** ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Delhi_Legislative_Assembly_election)).

## 3 · The April–May 2026 state cycle

### 3.1 West Bengal — the headline result

Polling on **23 and 29 April 2026** in two phases; counting on **4 May 2026**. The result ended 15 years of TMC rule:

| Party | Vote share | Seats (of 294) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| BJP | 45.13 % | majority |
| TMC | 40.97 % | runner-up |
| INC + Left | < 10 % | scattered |

**Turnout ≈ 93 %** — the **highest in any Indian state election in history** — on a roll roughly **11 % smaller than 2024's** after the ECI's **Special Intensive Revision (SIR)** ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_West_Bengal_Legislative_Assembly_election)).

**Mamata Banerjee** lost **Bhabanipur to Suvendu Adhikari** (who was also elected from **Nandigram** — the seat where he had beaten Mamata in 2021). **Adhikari sworn in as Chief Minister** — the **BJP's first CM in West Bengal** ([source](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Suvendu-Adhikari)).

#### The SIR controversy

The Special Intensive Revision of West Bengal's electoral rolls — conducted between October 2025 and March 2026 — **deleted approximately 9 million entries** (~12 % of the existing roll). The TMC alleged mass disenfranchisement of genuine voters, particularly in Muslim-majority constituencies. The BJP defended the SIR as the removal of bogus entries and illegal migrants. Several petitions filed at the Calcutta HC and the Supreme Court were pending at the time of polling; the elections proceeded under the revised roll.

### 3.2 Tamil Nadu — the TVK upset

Polling and results on **4 May 2026**. In what was widely called the most stunning state election upset in a generation, **actor C. Joseph Vijay's Tamilaga Vettri Kazhagam (TVK)** — a party he founded in February 2024 — **broke the DMK-AIADMK monopoly that had governed Tamil Nadu since 1967**.

| Party | Seats (of 234) |
|---:|---:|
| TVK | 108 |
| DMK + allies | ~70 |
| AIADMK + allies | ~50 |
| Others | rest |

**M.K. Stalin lost his Kolathur seat** and **resigned as Chief Minister on 5 May 2026** ([source](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/india/2026/05/04/tamil-nadu-assembly-election-results-2026-dmk-stalin-aiadmk-tvk-vijay-live.html)). Pre-poll and exit polls had under-estimated TVK by ~71 seats on average.

### 3.3 Kerala — UDF landslide

| Alliance | Seats (of 140) |
|---:|---:|
| UDF | 102 (INC alone 63) |
| LDF | 35 |

**Pinarayi Vijayan** retained Dharmadam by a narrow margin but **resigned**; 13 of 21 LDF ministers — including health minister Veena George — lost. Kerala's first **single-party majority** government in decades ([source](https://www.indiatvnews.com/news/india/kerala-assembly-election-results-2026-live-updates-left-ldf-congress-udf-leading-party-winning-party-pinarayi-vijayan-1039808)).

### 3.4 Assam — BJP returned

Polling on **9 April 2026**; results 4 May 2026.

| Alliance | Seats (of 126) |
|---:|---:|
| NDA (BJP-led) | 75 (BJP alone with allies: 82) |
| INC | worst-ever tally |

**Himanta Biswa Sarma** returned as Chief Minister ([source](https://www.indiatvnews.com/north-east/assam/assam-election-result-2026-live-updates-himanta-biswa-sarma-led-bjp-wins-setback-for-congress-1039454)).

### 3.5 Puducherry — AINRC alliance retained

The **AINRC + BJP + AIADMK alliance** crossed the majority mark in the 30 elected seats. **N. Rangasamy** retained his Thattanchavady seat and continued as Chief Minister ([source](https://www.indiatvnews.com/news/india/puducherry-election-results-ainrc-bjp-aiadmk-alliance-reaches-majority-mark-in-early-trends-2026-05-04-1039861)).

## 4 · Patterns across the cycle

### 4.1 The BJP's full ideological encirclement

By June 2026, the BJP has either independently or via NDA partners governed:

- **Hindi belt**: UP, MP, Rajasthan, Chhattisgarh, Haryana, Uttarakhand, Bihar.
- **West**: Maharashtra, Gujarat, Goa.
- **South**: AP (via TDP-led NDA), Karnataka (in opposition currently), Tamil Nadu (in opposition).
- **East**: **West Bengal (first time)**, Odisha, Tripura, Assam, Manipur.
- **North-east**: Arunachal, Nagaland (NDPP-led NDA), Sikkim (SKM-led NDA).
- **National Capital**: **Delhi (first time since 1998)**.

The states where the BJP and its allies remain in opposition: **Tamil Nadu (now TVK), Kerala (now UDF), Karnataka (INC), Himachal Pradesh (INC), Telangana (INC), Punjab (AAP), Jharkhand (INDIA), Jammu & Kashmir (NC+INC)**.

### 4.2 The southern push-back

The 2026 cycle confirmed that **southern India remains structurally outside BJP/NDA dominance**. The TVK upset in Tamil Nadu was the most striking instance, but Kerala's UDF wave and Telangana's continued Congress hold are part of the same pattern. The looming **post-2026 delimitation** — which would shift Lok Sabha seats *toward* the north — will intersect with this pattern in increasingly tense ways.

### 4.3 The end of the Modi-wave thesis

The 2024 LS reset showed that the BJP can lose its standalone majority. The 2026 WB win showed it can also expand into territory long thought hostile. Neither is "the Modi wave thesis" exactly — both fit a more granular reading: that **regional context, candidate selection, scheme delivery, and ground execution determine outcomes more than national narrative**.

## 5 · The constitutional consequences

Four constitutional consequences of the 2024–26 cycle:

1. **Coalition-era Lok Sabha**. After two terms of single-party BJP majority (2014, 2019), the 18th LS is **coalition-dependent**. This affects bill timetabling, committee-referral rates, and the use of ordinances.
2. **Formally recognised LOP**. For the first time since 2014, a Leader of Opposition can chair the Selection Committee for several constitutional appointments — most notably for the Lokpal, CIC, CVC, and (under the 2023 Act) the CEC and ECs.
3. **Centre-State frictions sharpen**. With major opposition-ruled states (TN, Kerala, Karnataka, HP, J&K, Punjab, Jharkhand) and one new BJP state (WB) all in play, the Governor question, the central agencies question, and the GST compensation question will be more contested in 2026–29 than at any time since the early 2000s.
4. **Delimitation politics + Women's Reservation operationalisation**. Both depend on the post-2026 census (scheduled 1 March 2027). The failed 131st Amendment in April 2026 is a preview of the politics to come.
