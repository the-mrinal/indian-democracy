# Local Self-Government: Panchayats & Nagar Palikas

<p class="lede">The third tier of Indian government — born of the 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendments in 1992 — is where citizen meets state most directly: in the <em>Gram Sabha</em> meeting in a village courtyard, in the Nagar Nigam's tax notice on your door, in the Ward Committee meeting that decides whether your street gets a streetlight. India has roughly <strong>2.5 lakh Panchayats</strong> at three tiers and over <strong>4,500 urban local bodies</strong>. On paper, this is the most decentralised democracy in the world. In practice, the devolution of <strong>3Fs — Functions, Funds and Functionaries</strong> — is still a work in progress.</p>

## 1 · Why a third tier?

For the first 45 years of the Republic, local self-government — though mentioned in Directive Principle Article 40 — was a state subject left to wide variation. Some states had vibrant Panchayats, others had none. The 73rd and 74th Amendments **constitutionalised** the third tier, making elections, reservations and basic structure non-negotiable.

The path to those amendments ran through four expert committees:

| Committee | Year | Key recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| **Balwant Rai Mehta** | 1957 | 3-tier "democratic decentralisation" — Gram Panchayat / Panchayat Samiti / Zila Parishad. Rajasthan adopted October 1959. |
| **Ashok Mehta** | 1977–78 | 2-tier (Mandal of 15–20k pop + Zila Parishad); constitutional recognition; party-based elections. |
| **G.V.K. Rao** | 1985 | Zila Parishad as pivotal unit; post of District Development Commissioner; regular elections. |
| **L.M. Singhvi** | 1986 | **Constitutional status for PRIs**; State Election Commissions. Directly informed the 73rd Amendment. |

## 2 · The 73rd Amendment, 1992 — Panchayati Raj

The **Constitution (Seventy-third Amendment) Act, 1992** was assented on 20 April 1993 and **came into force on 24 April 1993** — celebrated annually as **National Panchayati Raj Day**. It inserted **Part IX (Articles 243 to 243-O)** and the **Eleventh Schedule**.

States with a population of **less than 20 lakh** may dispense with the intermediate tier. So Goa and the smaller north-eastern states have only Village and District-level PRIs.

### 2.2 The Gram Sabha (Article 243A)

The **Gram Sabha** is the assembly of **all registered voters in a village**. It is not a representative body — it is the village's electorate itself, meeting in session. The Gram Sabha exercises powers that the state legislature assigns by law — typically including approval of plans/budgets, identification of beneficiaries, and social audit of works.

### 2.3 Elections (Article 243K)

- **State Election Commission** appointed by the Governor for each state.
- **Fixed 5-year term** for every Panchayat.
- Elections **must be held before expiry of the 5-year term** or within 6 months of dissolution.

### 2.4 Reservation (Article 243D)

- **SC/ST seats** reserved in proportion to their population in the Panchayat area.
- **Not less than one-third of total directly elected seats reserved for women** (calculated on the total, not residual).
- **Not less than one-third of SC/ST-reserved seats also reserved for SC/ST women**.
- **Chairperson posts reserved analogously** by rotation.

**20 states**, beginning with **Bihar in 2006**, have raised women's reservation in PRIs to **50 %** ([source](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/PrintRelease.aspx?relid=74501)) — Madhya Pradesh, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Kerala, Maharashtra, Odisha, Tripura, Tamil Nadu, Uttarakhand, Chhattisgarh, Rajasthan, Punjab, Sikkim, Telangana, West Bengal, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Gujarat, Jharkhand, Assam.

### 2.5 State Finance Commission (Article 243-I)

Constituted by the Governor within one year of the 73rd Amendment, and **every five years thereafter**. The SFC reviews the financial position of Panchayats and recommends:

- Distribution between the state and the Panchayats of taxes, duties, tolls and fees levied by the state.
- Determination of taxes, duties, tolls and fees assignable to Panchayats.
- Grants-in-aid to Panchayats from the Consolidated Fund of the State.

**Article 243-Y** extends the same SFC's mandate to Municipalities.

### 2.6 Eleventh Schedule — 29 subjects (Article 243G)

Subjects that **may** (not must) be devolved to Panchayats:

1. Agriculture, including agricultural extension
2. Land improvement, land reforms, land consolidation
3. Minor irrigation, water management, watershed development
4. Animal husbandry, dairying and poultry
5. Fisheries
6. Social forestry and farm forestry
7. Minor forest produce
8. Small-scale industries, including food processing
9. Khadi, village and cottage industries
10. Rural housing
11. Drinking water
12. Fuel and fodder
13. Roads, culverts, bridges, ferries, waterways and other means of communication
14. Rural electrification, including distribution of electricity
15. Non-conventional energy sources
16. Poverty alleviation programmes
17. Education, including primary and secondary schools
18. Technical training and vocational education
19. Adult and non-formal education
20. Libraries
21. Cultural activities
22. Markets and fairs
23. Health and sanitation, including hospitals, primary health centres and dispensaries
24. Family welfare
25. Women and child development
26. Social welfare, including welfare of the handicapped and mentally retarded
27. Welfare of the weaker sections, including SC/ST
28. Public distribution system
29. Maintenance of community assets

## 3 · The PESA Act, 1996

The **Provisions of the Panchayats (Extension to the Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996** — enacted **24 December 1996** — extends Part IX, with modifications, to **Fifth Schedule areas** to ensure self-governance through traditional Gram Sabhas.

PESA applies in **ten Fifth Schedule states**: **Andhra Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, Himachal Pradesh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, Rajasthan, Telangana** ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PESA_Act)).

Key PESA provisions:

- **Consultation** (not consent) on land acquisition.
- **Control** over minor water bodies and minor minerals.
- **Proportional ST reservation**.
- **ST chairpersons** in Panchayats located in Scheduled Areas.

PESA's promise is enormous; its implementation has been thin. The "consultation versus consent" loophole has been repeatedly criticised — most prominently in the context of forest-land diversions for mining and infrastructure ([source](https://www.impriindia.com/insights/panchayat-extension-scheduled-area-act/)).

## 4 · The 74th Amendment, 1992 — Municipalities

The **Constitution (Seventy-fourth Amendment) Act, 1992** **came into force on 1 June 1993**. It inserted **Part IX-A (Articles 243-P to 243-ZG)** and the **Twelfth Schedule**.

### 4.1 Three categories of urban local body

| Type | For |
|---|---|
| **Nagar Panchayat** | A "transitional area" — rural settlement transitioning to urban |
| **Municipal Council** | A smaller urban area |
| **Municipal Corporation** | A larger urban area |

The boundary criteria — population density, revenue, employment in non-agriculture — are set by state law.

### 4.2 Ward Committees (Article 243-S)

**Mandatory** in every Municipality with a population of **3 lakh (300,000) or more**. Composition and ward-territorial demarcation are determined by the State Legislature.

### 4.3 District Planning Committee (Article 243-ZD)

Consolidates plans prepared by Panchayats and Municipalities and prepares a draft development plan for the district as a whole.

### 4.4 Metropolitan Planning Committee (Article 243-ZE)

Mandatory in every Metropolitan Area. **At least two-thirds of members** must be elected from amongst elected representatives of Municipalities and Panchayat chairpersons within the area.

### 4.5 Twelfth Schedule — 18 subjects

Subjects that may be devolved to Municipalities ([source](https://www.education.gov.in/sites/upload_files/mhrd/files/upload_document/74amend.pdf)):

1. Urban planning, including town planning
2. Regulation of land use and construction of buildings
3. Planning for economic and social development
4. Roads and bridges
5. Water supply for domestic, industrial and commercial purposes
6. Public health, sanitation, conservancy and SWM
7. Fire services
8. Urban forestry, protection of the environment and ecology
9. Safeguarding the interests of weaker sections, including the handicapped and mentally retarded
10. Slum improvement and upgradation
11. Urban poverty alleviation
12. Provision of urban amenities (parks, gardens, playgrounds)
13. Promotion of cultural, educational and aesthetic aspects
14. Burials and cremation grounds; electric crematoria
15. Cattle pounds; prevention of cruelty to animals
16. Vital statistics, including registration of births and deaths
17. Public amenities — street lighting, parking lots, bus stops, public conveniences
18. Regulation of slaughter houses and tanneries

## 5 · The "3Fs" implementation gap

The 73rd/74th Amendments **mandated** structures, but the substantive devolution of the **3Fs — Functions, Funds, Functionaries** — was left to state legislatures. Actual devolution **varies sharply** across states.

| F | What it means | Reality |
|---|---|---|
| **Functions** | Subjects from 11th/12th Schedules actually transferred | Some states (Kerala, Karnataka) have devolved most; many large North Indian states have devolved little |
| **Funds** | Untied funds the local body can spend at its discretion | The least devolved. Local bodies remain heavily dependent on tied scheme grants and the Finance Commission. |
| **Functionaries** | Personnel actually under local body control | Limited — most "panchayat staff" remain state government employees seconded to PRIs |

Yamini Aiyar's work at the Accountability Initiative documents this through case studies of fund flows to Gram Panchayats — demonstrating chronic delays, fragmented grants, and weak own-source revenue ([source](https://accountabilityindia.in/team/yamini-aiyar/)).

## 6 · State Election Commissions

Distinct from the **Election Commission of India** (which conducts MP/MLA/Presidential/VP elections), each state has a **State Election Commission** under **Articles 243K (Panchayats) and 243ZA (Municipalities)**.

The State Election Commissioner is appointed by the Governor; removable only by the procedure for a High Court judge.

State Election Commissions:

- Prepare and publish local-body electoral rolls.
- Conduct Panchayat and Municipal elections.
- Resolve disputes related to local body elections (in many states).

## 7 · How a citizen actually participates

Three concrete ways:

1. **Vote** in your Gram Panchayat / Municipal election (every 5 years).
2. **Attend the Gram Sabha** (rural) or **Ward Committee meeting** (urban). The Gram Sabha is your direct channel; many social audits and beneficiary identifications must happen in open Gram Sabha.
3. **File RTIs** with your Panchayat / Municipal Corporation; demand information on works sanctioned, payments made, beneficiaries selected.

## 8 · The 15th Finance Commission and local bodies

The 15th FC (2021–26) recommended substantial grants to local bodies — roughly **₹4,36,361 crore** in total across rural and urban tiers — including **performance-based grants** linked to:

- Holding regular local body elections.
- Online publication of audited accounts.
- Sanitation and water supply outcomes.
- Property tax collection improvements.

The **16th FC (2026–31)** has signalled it will retain the broad framework while strengthening the performance-grant linkage.

## 9 · An honest evaluation

After more than three decades, the verdict is mixed:

- **Wins**: regular elections at all three tiers; women's substantive participation (~1.4 million women elected to PRIs and ULBs nationwide); explicit constitutional protection from arbitrary supersession; growing fiscal flows.
- **Gaps**: thin actual devolution of 3Fs; capture by local elites in many regions; weak audit and accountability; chronic mismatch between responsibilities and resources; weak own-source revenue.

The **next constitutional moment** for local government is likely to involve a **fresh push for substantive devolution** — and possibly a constitutional amendment to put fiscal floors under Panchayats and Municipalities (as opposed to the current discretionary State Finance Commission model).
