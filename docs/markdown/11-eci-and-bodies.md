# Election Commission & Constitutional Bodies

<p class="lede">The Constitution creates a handful of <strong>independent constitutional bodies</strong> — beyond the three branches of government — to do specific structural jobs the State must do but the political executive cannot be trusted to do. The Election Commission. The UPSC. The Finance Commission. The CAG. The NCSC/NCST/NCBC. Each has constitutional protection of independence, but each has had its independence tested.</p>

## 1 · The Election Commission of India (Article 324)

### 1.1 What it does

Article 324 vests in the Election Commission of India "**the superintendence, direction and control** of the preparation of the electoral rolls for, and the conduct of, all elections to:

- the Parliament of India (Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha)
- the Legislatures of every State (Vidhan Sabha + Vidhan Parishad where applicable)
- the office of **President**
- the office of **Vice-President**" ([source](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/articles/article-324-superintendence-direction-and-control-of-elections-to-be-vested-in-an-election-commission/)).

In ***Mohinder Singh Gill v. CEC* (1978)**, the Supreme Court read these powers as **plenary** — operating in any area not occupied by legislation, so that the ECI can fill statutory gaps to ensure free and fair elections.

(Local body elections — Panchayats and Municipalities — are conducted by the separately constituted **State Election Commissions** under Articles 243K and 243ZA, not by the ECI.)

### 1.2 Composition — single → multi → single → multi

A long, peculiar trajectory:

- **1950–1989**: single-member ECI (only the CEC).
- **16 October 1989**: Rajiv Gandhi government made it multi-member — S.S. Dhanoa and V.S. Seigell sworn in as two ECs.
- **1 January 1990**: V.P. Singh government **reverted** to single-member by abolishing the EC posts.
- **1 October 1993**: Multi-member structure made **permanent** — two ECs appointed alongside CEC **T.N. Seshan**. Decisions taken by majority vote ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_Commission_of_India)).

### 1.3 Tenure and removal

- **Tenure**: **6 years or 65 years, whichever is earlier**.
- **Salary and conditions** equivalent to a Supreme Court judge.
- **CEC removal**: by the same procedure as a Supreme Court judge — special majority in both Houses on grounds of proved misbehaviour or incapacity.
- **EC removal**: **only on the CEC's recommendation** to the President — a much weaker protection.

### 1.4 Appointment — the 2023 statutory change

For decades, the President made the appointments on the **advice of the Prime Minister** — an executive monopoly.

In ***Anoop Baranwal v. Union of India* (2 March 2023)**, a 5-judge Constitution Bench (J. K.M. Joseph leading) unanimously held that, **until Parliament enacts a law**, the President should appoint the CEC and ECs on the recommendation of a **3-member committee: PM + Leader of Opposition (LS) + Chief Justice of India** ([source](https://theamikusqriae.com/anoop-baranwal-v-union-of-india-election-commission-appointments-2023/)).

The Government's response: the **CEC and Other ECs (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, 2023** (notified 28 December 2023). The Act:

- **Replaces the CJI on the committee with a Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the PM** — giving the executive a 2:1 majority.
- A **Search Committee** headed by the Cabinet Secretary shortlists 5 names.
- The committee's recommendations remain valid **even when the LoP seat is vacant**.
- Status retained at SC-judge equivalence; tenure 6 years or 65 years.

Petitions challenging the Act are pending at the Supreme Court; interim stay refused ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Election_Commissioner_and_Other_Election_Commissioners_(Appointment,_Conditions_of_Service_and_Term_of_Office)_Act,_2023)).

### 1.6 Field machinery

Below the Commission:

- **Chief Electoral Officer (CEO)** at the **state** level — typically a senior IAS officer.
- **District Election Officer (DEO)** at the **district** level — usually the District Magistrate.
- **Electoral Registration Officer (ERO)** at the **Assembly Constituency** level — maintains the electoral roll.
- **Returning Officer (RO)** + **Assistant ROs** — conduct the election in each constituency.
- **Presiding Officer + Polling Officers** — staff each polling booth on poll day.
- **Booth Level Officer (BLO)** — typically a teacher, anganwadi worker, panchayat secretary, or village-level worker — keeps the electoral roll current on a booth-by-booth basis.

## 2 · The Union Public Service Commission (Articles 315–323)

### 2.1 What it does

The UPSC is the **central recruiting agency** for the All India Services (IAS, IPS, IFoS) and Central Civil Services (e.g., IRS, IFS, IAAS, IRTS, IDAS, IPoS, IPS Central). It conducts annual recruitment examinations — most famously the **Civil Services Examination** — and advises on promotions, disciplinary matters and pension entitlements of central services personnel.

### 2.2 Composition

- **Chairman + Members** appointed by the **President** under Article 316.
- **Tenure**: **6 years or 65 years, whichever is earlier**.
- **At least half** the members must have at least **10 years of central or state government service**.

### 2.3 Removal (Article 317)

The President may remove the Chairman or a Member only on grounds of:

- Misbehaviour or incapacity (after a reference to the Supreme Court and on the basis of its inquiry/report);
- Insolvency;
- Paid employment outside the office;
- Infirmity of mind or body.

### 2.4 State Public Service Commissions

Constituted for each State under Article 315 with the same structure. Chair and Members appointed by the **Governor**; removal by the **President** (on SC reference). Tenure for state PSC members: **6 years or 62 years**, whichever earlier.

## 3 · The Finance Commission (Article 280)

### 3.1 What it does

Constituted by the **President every five years** (or earlier as needed) to recommend:

1. **Distribution of net proceeds of taxes** between the Centre and the States (vertical and horizontal devolution);
2. **Principles governing grants-in-aid** to States out of the Consolidated Fund (Art. 275);
3. **Measures to augment the Consolidated Fund of a State** to supplement the resources of Panchayats and Municipalities;
4. Any other matter referred by the President.

It is a **quasi-judicial body** — its recommendations are advisory but, by convention, almost always accepted.

### 3.2 Recent commissions

- **14th FC** (Y.V. Reddy, 2015–20) — raised the **states' share of the divisible pool from 32 % to 42 %**.
- **15th FC** (N.K. Singh, **2021–26**) — set the share at **41 %**. Single percentage-point drop reflects the carve-out of J&K and Ladakh as UTs in 2019.
- **16th FC** (Arvind Panagariya, **2026–31**) — constituted 31 December 2023. Report tabled in Parliament on **1 February 2026**. **Recommended retaining the 41 % share** for states ([source](https://www.legacyias.com/16th-finance-commission-2026-31-upsc-notes/)).

### 3.3 Horizontal allocation

Within the states' share, allocation is by formula. Recent formulas have weighted population (1971 vs 2011), income distance, area, forest cover, demographic performance, and tax effort. The exact weights have been politically charged — particularly the partial switch from 1971 to 2011 census data, which favours high-fertility northern states.

## 4 · The Comptroller and Auditor General (Article 148)

### 4.1 What it does

The **CAG** audits the accounts of the Union, the States, and bodies substantially financed by the government. Its reports are tabled in Parliament/State legislatures, where they are examined primarily by the **Public Accounts Committee**.

Dr. B.R. Ambedkar called the CAG "the most important officer under the Constitution of India" and the "guardian of the public purse."

### 4.2 Appointment and tenure

- **Appointed by the President**.
- **Tenure**: **6 years or 65 years**, whichever earlier.
- **Removal**: by the same procedure as a Supreme Court judge.
- **Salary and conditions of service** are charged on the **Consolidated Fund of India** and cannot be varied to the holder's disadvantage.

### 4.3 Current CAG (June 2026)

**K. Sanjay Murthy** — 1989-batch IAS, Himachal Pradesh cadre. Took oath on **21 November 2024**, succeeding G.C. Murmu (8 August 2020 – 20 November 2024).

## 5 · National Commissions for SCs, STs, BCs

A trio of constitutional commissions, each Chairperson + Vice-Chairperson + 3 Members appointed by the President for 3 years (max 2 terms). Each has **civil-court powers** for investigation and submits annual reports to the President, who lays them before Parliament.

| Body | Article | Created |
|---|---|---|
| **National Commission for Scheduled Castes (NCSC)** | Art. 338 | Originally combined; separated by 89th Amendment, 2003 |
| **National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (NCST)** | Art. 338A | 89th Amendment, 2003 |
| **National Commission for Backward Classes (NCBC)** | Art. 338B | **102nd Amendment, 2018** — previously statutory under NCBC Act, 1993 |

The **102nd Amendment** also inserted **Article 342A**, empowering the President — on consultation with the Governor — to specify Socially and Educationally Backward Classes (SEBCs) for each State/UT. After the *Maratha quota* case, the **105th Amendment (2021)** restored states' power to identify their own SEBCs.

## 6 · The Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities (Article 350B)

Added by the **7th Amendment, 1956**, on the recommendation of the States Reorganisation Commission, 1955.

- Appointed by the **President**.
- Investigates matters relating to safeguards for linguistic minorities.
- Reports to the President via the Ministry of Minority Affairs; reports laid before each House of Parliament and circulated to State governments.
- Headquartered in **Allahabad**, with regional offices in **Belgaum, Chennai and Kolkata**.

## 7 · The Attorney General & State Advocates General

Already covered in Chapters 5 and 9, but worth recapping here:

| Office | Article | Appointment | Tenure | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Attorney General of India** | Art. 76 | By the President | During President's pleasure | Highest law officer; right of audience in all courts; can speak in Parliament but not vote |
| **Solicitor General + ASGs** | Statutory (Law Officers Rules) | By Appointments Committee of Cabinet | Co-terminus with AG / fixed term | Assist AG; speak for the Government |
| **State Advocate General** | Art. 165 | By the Governor | During Governor's pleasure | State's chief law officer; must be qualified to be a High Court judge |

## 8 · Other important non-constitutional bodies

Although not constitutional bodies, several **statutory** and **executive** bodies are routinely included in the architecture you should know:

- **NITI Aayog** — replaced the Planning Commission in 2015. Think-tank and "co-operative federalism" forum. Chaired by the PM.
- **Lokpal of India** — constituted under the Lokpal & Lokayuktas Act, 2013; investigates corruption complaints against public servants including the PM (with safeguards).
- **National Human Rights Commission (NHRC)** — constituted under the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993.
- **Central Vigilance Commission (CVC)** — statutory body under the CVC Act, 2003; oversees vigilance and anti-corruption work in central government.
- **Central Information Commission (CIC)** — under the RTI Act, 2005.
- **Reserve Bank of India** — under the RBI Act, 1934; the central bank.
- **Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)** — capital markets regulator.
- **National Commission for Women (NCW)** — statutory.
