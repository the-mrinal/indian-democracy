# Research Notes — Local Government, Parties, Anti-Defection, Ground Execution

*Source: research agent run, June 2026. Used to draft chapters 14 (Local Govt), 15 (Parties & Anti-Defection), 16 (Money in Politics), 17 (Ground Execution).*

## Pre-73rd Committees
- Balwant Rai Mehta (1957) — recommended 3-tier indirectly elected; Rajasthan adopted Oct 1959
- Ashok Mehta (1977–78) — 132 recommendations Aug 1978; 2-tier (Mandal 15–20k pop + ZP); constitutional recognition; statutorily mandated party participation
- G.V.K. Rao (1985) — ZP as pivotal unit, post of DDC, regular elections
- L.M. Singhvi (1986) — constitutional recognition; State Election Commissions; coined "PRI" framing

## 73rd Amendment 1992 (assented 20 Apr 1993, in force 24 Apr 1993)
- Added Part IX (Arts 243 – 243-O) + 11th Schedule
- 3-tier (Art 243B): GP village → PS/Mandal/Block intermediate → ZP district
- Intermediate tier exempt for states <20 lakh pop
- Gram Sabha (Art 243A): body of all registered voters in a village
- Elections (Art 243K): State Election Commission appointed by Governor; 5-year fixed term
- Reservation (Art 243D): SC/ST proportional to population; ≥1/3 women on total; ≥1/3 of SC/ST reserved for SC/ST women; chairperson posts reserved analogously
- 20 states have ≥50% women's reservation in PRIs (Bihar first in 2006; also MP, Karnataka)
- State Finance Commission (Art 243-I): every 5 years; also covers municipalities (Art 243-Y)
- 11th Schedule (Art 243G) — 29 subjects: agriculture, land improvement, minor irrigation, animal husbandry, fisheries, social forestry, MFP, small scale industries, khadi/village/cottage industries, rural housing, drinking water, fuel/fodder, roads/bridges, rural electrification, NCE, poverty alleviation, education (primary/secondary), technical/vocational training, adult/non-formal education, libraries, cultural activities, markets/fairs, health/sanitation, family welfare, women/child development, social welfare incl. handicapped/mentally retarded, weaker sections incl SC/ST, PDS, maintenance of community assets

## PESA Act 1996 (24 Dec 1996)
- Extends Part IX with modifications to Fifth Schedule areas
- Self-governance through traditional Gram Sabhas
- 10 Fifth Schedule states: AP, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, HP, Jharkhand, MP, Maharashtra, Odisha, Rajasthan, Telangana
- Consultation (NOT consent) on land acquisition; control of minor water bodies + minerals; proportional ST reservation; ST chairpersons in Scheduled Areas Panchayats
- Critiques: weak devolution, "consultation vs consent" loophole, admin inertia

## 74th Amendment 1992 (in force 1 Jun 1993)
- Added Part IX-A (Arts 243-P to 243-ZG) + 12th Schedule
- 3 categories: Nagar Panchayat (transitional), Municipal Council (smaller urban), Municipal Corporation (larger urban)
- Ward Committees (Art 243-S): mandatory for ULBs ≥3 lakh pop
- District Planning Committee (Art 243-ZD)
- Metropolitan Planning Committee (Art 243-ZE): ≥2/3 elected from Municipality + Panchayat chairs
- 12th Schedule — 18 subjects: urban planning incl town planning; land use & building reg; planning for econ/social devp; roads/bridges; water supply (dom/ind/comm); public health/sanitation/conservancy/SWM; fire services; urban forestry/env/ecology; safeguarding weaker sections incl handicapped/mentally retarded; slum improvement/upgradation; urban poverty alleviation; urban amenities (parks/gardens/playgrounds); cultural/educational/aesthetic; burials/cremations; cattle pounds/cruelty prevention; vital statistics (births/deaths registration); public amenities (street lighting, parking lots, bus stops, public conveniences); slaughter houses & tanneries regulation

## 3Fs Implementation Gap
- Functions, Funds, Functionaries — devolution varies by state
- Funds = least devolved; PRIs depend on tied scheme grants vs untied
- Yamini Aiyar (Accountability Initiative) — chronic delays, fragmented grants, weak own-source revenue

## Party Registration — Sec 29A RPA 1951
- Apply to ECI within 30 days of formation
- Publish proposed name in 2 national + 2 local dailies inviting objections within 30 days
- Required for Sec 13A IT benefits + symbol allotment
- Registration ≠ recognition
- PPRTMS launched Jan 2020 for SMS/email tracking

## Recognition (Symbols Order 1968)
- National: 6% valid votes in 4+ states (LS/Assembly) + 4 LS seats; OR 2% of LS seats (~11) from 3+ states; OR State Party status in 4+ states
- State: 6% in Assembly + 2 seats; OR 6% in LS from state + 1 LS seat; OR 3% of Assembly seats or 3 seats (higher); OR 8% of valid votes in state
- Benefits: reserved symbol, free DD/AIR time, consultation on dates/rules, enhanced star campaigner quota

## Party Funding
- IT Act Sec 13A: total exemption on house property, capital gains, other sources, voluntary contributions IF maintains books, audits, records donors >₹20k (except via electoral bonds), accepts no donation >₹2k except cheque/draft/electronic/bond, files ITR
- Sec 29C RPA: annual contribution report (donations >₹20k) to ECI

## Electoral Bonds (2018) — struck down 15 Feb 2024
- SC Constitution Bench (ADR v UoI) — donor anonymity violates Art 19(1)(a)
- SBI ordered to disclose all bond purchases from 12 Apr 2019; ECI published 15 Mar 2024
- Companies Act Sec 154 (Finance Act 2017) had removed 7.5% corp donation cap + deleted disclosure — ALSO struck down → restored pre-2017 7.5% cap + disclosure

## Star Campaigners — Sec 77 RPA 1951
- Travel by "leaders of party" NOT counted in candidate's expenses
- Recognised party: up to 40 star campaigners
- Unrecognised registered: up to 20
- If star campaigner solicits votes for specific candidate or shares dais → meeting cost apportioned
- If candidate travels with star campaigner → 50% of travel apportioned

## Candidate Expenditure Limits (Jan 2022)
- LS: ₹95 lakh (large) / ₹75 lakh (small)
- Assembly: ₹40 lakh (large) / ₹28 lakh (small)
- Centre for Media Studies estimate: total 2024 LS expenditure ~₹1,00,000 crore (some experts ₹1.35 lakh crore — costliest election in world history)

## Anti-Defection (10th Schedule) — 52nd Amdt 1985

### Grounds
1. Voluntarily giving up party membership (including by conduct)
2. Voting/abstaining against party whip (without permission, condoned within 15 days)
3. Independent joining a party after election
4. Nominated member joining a party >6 months after taking seat

### 91st Amdt 2003
- Deleted "split" exception (was 1/3)
- Only "merger" exception: 2/3 of legislature party PLUS original party formally merging

### Kihoto Hollohan v Zachillhu (18 Feb 1992, 5-judge bench, 3:2)
- Upheld 10th Schedule's validity
- Struck down Paragraph 7 (ousting judicial review) — needed state ratification under Art 368(2)
- Speaker functions as Tribunal under 10th Schedule; decisions reviewable

### Keisham Meghachandra Singh v Speaker, Manipur (21 Jan 2020)
- 3-month outer limit (judicial guidance, NOT statutorily binding)
- Urged independent tribunal of retired judges instead of partisan Speaker

### Recent landmarks
- **Karnataka 2019**: 17 (not 15) MLAs disqualified; SC upheld disqualification but set aside Speaker's bar from contesting until assembly end (Speaker has no power to prescribe duration)
- **Maharashtra Shiv Sena — Subhash Desai v Governor (11 May 2023, 5-judge)**: Governor's call for floor test unjustified absent objective material; couldn't restore Thackeray (already resigned). Speaker (not ECI symbol order) decides "real" legislature party.
- **NCP split (Jul 2023)**: Ajit Pawar with 41/53 MLAs. Speaker Rahul Narwekar (Feb 2024) held Ajit faction = "real NCP"; defying party founder ≠ defection. ECI on 6 Feb 2024 allotted "clock" symbol to Ajit faction.
- **Telangana 2024**: 10 BRS MLAs defected to Cong Mar-Apr 2024. Speaker Gaddam Prasad Kumar dismissed petitions for lack of evidence. SC Jul 2025 directed 3 months. Contempt notices Nov 2025; further 3-week extension Feb 2026.

## Women's Reservation (Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam)
- 106th Amdt 2023; introduced LS 19 Sept 2023; passed LS 20 Sept (454-2); passed RS 21 Sept (214-0)
- New Arts 330A, 332A: 1/3 reservation LS + every SLA + Delhi LA; 1/3 within SC/ST reserved
- Art 334A: operational only after delimitation post first census after Act commencement
- Centre notified force 16 Apr 2026; first census scheduled 1 Mar 2027; earliest application 2029 LS
- Critiques: long deferral, constituency-MP relationship disruption via rotation, no OBC sub-reservation, symbolic vs substantive

## Shivam Shankar Singh — How to Win an Indian Election (Penguin 2019)
- Author: BJP data analytics for Manipur + Tripura; brief IPAC stint in Punjab

### Booth as smallest unit
- BLO = ECI's booth-level officer (teacher, anganwadi, panchayat secretary, VLW) — maintains electoral roll
- BLA = party's booth-level agent; accompanies BLO on door-to-door enumeration; operationally = polling agent
- Controlling BLA network = controlling electoral roll at granular level

### Panna Pramukh model (BJP)
- One karyakarta assigned ONE double-sided page of electoral roll (~60 names)
- Responsible from voter-list verification through GOTV
- Central to BJP dominance from 2014

### Booth classification
- Friendly / hostile / swing — based on past patterns, caste arithmetic, surveys
- Resource allocation: GOTV+transport for friendly; suppression/contest for hostile; saturation messaging for swing
- "Parchi vitran" = slip distribution with booth, serial, section by BLA network

### Campaign budget reality
- Official ₹95 lakh LS limit vs actual spending of several crores routinely
- Where money goes: cash to booth workers, vehicles/fuel, food, posters/hoardings, social media/IT cell salaries, paid surveys, helicopter logistics, envelope politics

### Surveys & microtargeting
- Baseline + tracking + internal exit poll
- Voter rolls merged with mobile numbers, DBT beneficiary lists (PMAY, Ujjwala, PMJDY, PM-KISAN, Ladli Behna)
- Caste/community enumeration by panna pramukh + BLA
- WhatsApp groups, FB/Twitter campaigns, IT-cell troll armies — extended in follow-up book on disinformation

### Cambridge Analytica
- 2018 — Ovleno Business Intelligence (OBI), CA's India partner, listed BJP, INC, JD(U) as clients
- OBI MD Amrish Tyagi = son of JD(U) leader K.C. Tyagi
- SCL India = OBI x SCL Group London JV

### Other mobilisation architectures
- TMC: "Banglar Garbo Mamata" outreach
- DMK: volunteer cadre
- "Vote contractors" + local strongmen for fixed consideration

### Election day & post
- Vehicle deployment for friendly booths (within bounds)
- Polling agents: 1 principal + relief per booth per candidate
- Counting agents at every counting table
- Post-result thank-you tours + rewards for loyal booth karyakartas
- Booth capturing now rare (EVMs, VVPATs, CCTV, CAPF) — but local violence persists

### State machinery & scheme branding
- Incumbency = PMAY, PMJDY, Ujjwala, JJM, PM-KISAN, Ladli Behna — outreach list + delivery claim
- Challenger counter: "victim card" framing of caste/community exclusion

## Milan Vaishnav — When Crime Pays (Yale 2017)
- Demand-side theory: in state-weak contexts, voters reward candidates who deliver goods/justice/protection
- Criminal record signals capacity — especially in fragmented multi-party contests with co-ethnic mobilisation
- Parties select criminal candidates because: self-financing + winnable

### 2024 LS Data (ADR)
- 251/543 (46%) declared criminal cases; 27 convicted
- 170/543 (31%) declared SERIOUS cases (rape, murder, attempt to murder, kidnapping, crimes against women)
- +55% MPs with criminal cases since 2009; +124% with serious cases
- BJP 63 (26%), Congress 32 (32%), SP 17 (46%) serious cases
- 93% crorepatis; only 14% women
- Candidates with criminal cases had HIGHER winnability than clean candidates

## Cross-cutting critiques
1. Devolution gap (Kerala/Karnataka most devolved; large N. Indian states least)
2. Anti-defection — Speaker partiality
3. Money in politics — no successor transparency to bonds
4. Women's reservation deferral — at earliest 2029
