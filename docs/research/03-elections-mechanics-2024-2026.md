# Research Notes — Elections Mechanics & Recent Results

*Source: research agent run, June 2026. Used to draft chapters 11 (ECI), 12 (How Elections Work), 13 (Electoral Math), 18 (Recent Elections).*

## ECI Architecture
- Art 324: superintendence, direction, control over elections to Parliament, Legislatures, President, VP
- Plenary powers (Mohinder Singh Gill v CEC, 1978)
- Multi-member history: 16 Oct 1989 multi → 1 Jan 1990 single → 1 Oct 1993 permanent multi
- 2023 Act details (see 02 notes)
- Current (2026): CEC Gyanesh Kumar, EC Sukhbir Singh Sandhu, EC Dr. Vivek Joshi

## Election Calendar & MCC
- MCC effective on schedule announcement → result declaration
- MCC NOT a statute; ECI enforces via moral sanction under Art 324. Statutory underpins: BNS, Sec 123 RPA 1951 (corrupt practices)
- Restricts: new schemes, transfers, ruling-party ads, communal/caste appeals, religious places for campaign
- Sec 126 RPA: silence period 48 hrs before poll close

## Phased Polling
- 2024 LS: 7 phases, 19 Apr – 1 Jun 2024; counting 4 Jun 2024
- Pacing depends on CAPF availability

## Polling Infrastructure
- 2024: 1.048 million polling stations; 5.5 million EVMs; ~15M election workers + security
- ECI norm: polling station ≤2 km from voter
- Aug 2024: ECI raised electors-per-booth from 1,200 → 1,500; challenged in SC

## EVMs and VVPAT
- 1982: EVMs first used in Paravur Assembly bye-election, Kerala (50 of 123 booths)
- 1984: SC struck down (no statute then)
- 1989/1992: Parliament amended RPA + Conduct of Election Rules to legitimise EVMs
- 1998: phased re-introduction
- 2004 GE: all-EVM polling
- VVPAT: SC mandate 8 Oct 2013 (Subramanian Swamy v ECI); paired with every EVM by 2019 GE
- N. Chandrababu Naidu v UoI (8 Apr 2019): mandatory VVPAT verification raised from 1 to 5 random polling stations per AC

## Postal Ballots & ETPBS
- Service voters → ETPBS (e-delivered blank ballot, printed, marked, physically returned)
- 2024: home voting rolled out NATIONWIDE for 85+ and PwDs (≥40% benchmark). Form 12D within 5 days of notification; team visits; video-recorded.

## Electoral Roll & Voter ID
- EPIC = Electors Photo Identity Card
- Form 6 (new), Form 6A (overseas), Form 7 (objection/deletion), Form 8 (correction/shifting/PwD)
- 2024 GE: ~969M registered voters; 642M voted

## NOTA
- SC direction 27 Sept 2013 (PUCL v UoI, (2013) 10 SCC 1)
- Secrecy of ballot extends to those choosing nobody; pushes parties to nominate cleaner candidates

## Expenditure Ceilings (Jan 2022 revision)
- LS: ₹95 lakh (large states), ₹75 lakh (small)
- Assembly: ₹40 lakh (large), ₹28 lakh (small)
- NO STATUTORY CAP on party expenditure — long-standing loophole

## Electoral Bonds — Birth & Death
- Announced Jaitley 2017-18 Budget; notified 2 Jan 2018; first tranche Mar 2018
- Bearer bonds at SBI in ₹1k / ₹10k / ₹1L / ₹10L / ₹1cr; donor identity hidden
- Also removed corporate cap of 7.5% of avg 3-yr net profits
- **15 Feb 2024**: 5-judge SC bench (CJI Chandrachud + Khanna + Gavai + Pardiwala + Misra) UNANIMOUSLY struck down — violates Art 19(1)(a) right to info
- 11 Mar 2024: SC ordered SBI disclose donor/recipient
- 15 Mar 2024: ECI published data on website (Apr 2019–Jan 2024 bonds)
- Top donors revealed: Megha Engineering, Vedanta, Bharti Airtel, Grasim. BJP largest beneficiary, then TMC, Congress, BRS.

## Electoral Systems
- FPTP: LS, State Assemblies, Panchayats, Municipalities
- STV-PR: RS, LCs, President, VP

## Vote-Share vs Seat-Share Anomalies
- BJP 2019: ~37.4% national vote → 303 LS seats
- Congress 2014: 19.3% → 44 seats (worst-ever); UPA total 59 < 55-seat LoP threshold
- Regional concentration: DMK, TMC, BJD convert vote → seat efficiently; BSP wastes diffuse vote

## Delimitation
- 4 Commissions: 1952, 1962, 1973, 2002
- 42nd Amdt 1976: froze state-wise on 1971 census
- 84th Amdt 2001: extended freeze to first census after 2026
- 87th Amdt 2003: intra-state boundary changes on 2001 census, totals unchanged
- 16 Apr 2026: Constitution (131st) Bill + UT Laws Amdt + Delimitation Bill introduced to enable post-2026 delimitation. (131st failed 298–230 short of 352 needed). Status quo continues.

## SC/ST Reservation Extension
- 104th Amdt (notified 25 Jan 2020): extended SC/ST reservation in LS+SLAs to 25 Jan 2030 (substituted "eighty years" for "seventy years" in Art 334)
- Same amdt ended Anglo-Indian nomination (Art 331 abolished)

## Women's Reservation — Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam
- Constitution (106th) Amdt Act 2023: 1/3 reservation for women in LS, SLAs, Delhi Legislative Assembly; includes 1/3 within SC/ST reserved
- LS passed 20 Sept 2023 (454–2); RS 21 Sept (214–0); President assent 28 Sept 2023
- Art 334A: operational AFTER delimitation post first census after commencement
- Centre notified law into force **16 Apr 2026**
- First census scheduled **1 Mar 2027**
- Earliest realistic application: **2029 LS** (possibly later)
- 15-yr duration, extendable

## Party Recognition
- Symbols Order 1968:
  - National party: 4+ states recognition OR 6% valid votes in 4 states (LS/Assembly) + 4 LS seats OR 2% of LS seats from 3+ states
- ECI 10 Apr 2023 review:
  - National parties: BJP, INC, CPI(M), BSP, NPP, AAP (6)
  - LOST national status: CPI, NCP, TMC
- Reserved symbol nationally, free AIR/DD time, free electoral rolls, 40 star campaigners (20 for unrecognised)

## Anti-Defection (10th Schedule)
- 52nd Amdt 1985
- 91st Amdt 2003: REMOVED "split" exception (was 1/3); only "merger" exception remains, requiring 2/3 of legislature party
- Speaker/Chairman adjudicates; subject to judicial review (Kihoto Hollohan v Zachillhu 1992)
- Recent:
  - **Shiv Sena split (Jun 2022)**: Shinde led 37/55 MLAs out. 10 Jan 2024 Speaker held Shinde faction = "real Shiv Sena", declining disqualification — criticized as defeating spirit of law.
  - **NCP split (Jul 2023)**: Ajit Pawar led 41/53 MLAs to govt. 15 Feb 2024 Speaker held Ajit Pawar faction = "real NCP", reasoning anti-defection cannot stifle dissent.
  - In both, ECI allotted party name+symbol to breakaway. Original leaders left with "Shiv Sena (UBT)" and "NCP (SP)".
  - Keisham Meghachandra Singh (2020) — SC suggested 3-month limit on Speaker; not statutorily binding.
